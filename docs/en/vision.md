# 1. Vision and Design Principles

## Background

Major earthquakes damage not only homes but also roads, bridges, water and sewer systems, disaster-response facilities, and other infrastructure essential to daily life. Recovery takes years and requires sustained support and accountability after immediate public attention fades.

For supporters, especially those overseas, it is difficult to continuously verify whether their contribution reached an authorized recipient and how it was subsequently used for restoration.

## Objectives

Kumamoto Relief DAO seeks to achieve three goals simultaneously:

1. Enable people in Japan and abroad to participate in recovery support with minimal friction.
2. Make the path of support to a destination designated by Kumamoto Prefecture publicly verifiable.
3. Continuously communicate recovery progress back to supporters.

## The tamagaki metaphor

A shrine's tamagaki is a sacred fence that surrounds the grounds and preserves donors' names over time. This project issues a non-transferable digital tamagaki for each contribution and presents the collective support as a landscape surrounding Kumamoto Castle.

The Tamagaki SBT is not a membership right, investment product, or repayment claim. It is a non-financial digital keepsake recording participation and the stages through which the contribution has passed.

## Design principles

- **Recovery first** — Center infrastructure restoration and regional resilience.
- **Separate funds from voting** — Limit DAO-style participation to expressing views; it conveys no authority to move funds.
- **Traceability** — Distinguish amounts received, converted to yen, remitted, pending, held, and charged as fees.
- **Minimal on-chain data** — Record only values and hashes required for public verification.
- **Phased implementation** — Progress from a safe demo to testnet validation, stakeholder agreement, and production audit.

## Out of scope

- Furusato Nozei, donation deductions, or tax-credit certificates
- Relief money distributed directly to individual disaster victims
- Investment returns, dividends, or token appreciation
- DAO control over public budgets, procurement, or construction priorities
